# 1. AWKWARD

## FRONT

**awkward**

“I felt awkward asking for help.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

Feeling uneasy, self‑conscious, or uncomfortable in a situation.

**Category:**

adjective

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/ˈɔːkwɚd/ (General American) – stress on the first syllable; the /kw/ cluster is pronounced like the 'qu' in 'quick'.

**More examples:**

- She looked awkward when she realized everyone was watching her presentation.
- It was awkward when I ran into my ex at the grocery store.
- He gave an awkward laugh after his joke fell flat.
- The silence after the mistake felt awkward for everyone in the room.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- feel awkward about
- awkward silence
- awkward moment
- make someone feel awkward
- awkward situation
- awkwardly

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Avoid using 'awkward' to describe a person’s character; it should refer to a specific feeling or moment. For example, say 'He felt awkward' rather than 'He is awkward' unless you mean he is socially clumsy.

**Natural alternatives:**

- uncomfortable
- self‑conscious
- ill at ease
- embarrassed

# 2. DRAINING

## FRONT

**draining**

“Back-to-back meetings can be draining.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

Very tiring or exhausting; it saps your energy or enthusiasm.

**Category:**

adjective

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/ˈdreɪ.nɪŋ/ (stress on the first syllable, the “dr” is like in “dream”, the “a” is the long A sound)

**More examples:**

- The long flight was draining, and I fell asleep as soon as I got home.
- Working on that project for three weeks straight was completely draining.
- His constant complaining is draining for everyone in the office.
- After the marathon, the crowd felt draining, but the runners were thrilled.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- draining experience
- draining job
- draining conversation
- draining schedule
- draining workload

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Do not mix this figurative sense with the literal meaning of removing liquid (e.g., “draining the bathtub”). In the figurative sense it describes mental or physical fatigue, not actual fluid removal.

**Natural alternatives:**

- exhausting
- tiring
- fatiguing
- wearing

# 3. WENT

## FRONT

**went**

“I went to the office yesterday.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

past simple of “go”; it shows that the speaker moved to a place in the past.

**Category:**

verb

**Irregular Forms:**

go → went → gone

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/wɛnt/ (stress on the only syllable; the final /t/ is released)

**More examples:**

- She went home early because she felt sick.
- We went over the budget numbers in the meeting.
- After the concert, they went out for drinks.
- The package went missing during transit.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- went ahead
- went missing
- went out
- went over
- went through

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Learners often say \*“I went to go …”\* which is redundant. Use either \*went\* alone (past) or \*went to\* + infinitive for purpose, e.g., \*I went to the store to buy milk.\*

**Natural alternatives:**

- traveled to
- headed to
- drove to
- made my way to

# 4. PUT OFF

## FRONT

**put off**

“Let's not put off this conversation.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

to delay or postpone something, especially a discussion, meeting, or task

**Category:**

phrasal verb

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/pʊt ɔf/ (stress on the first word; the /t/ is released, the /f/ is unvoiced)

**More examples:**

- We kept putting off the budget review until the deadline passed.
- She decided to put off her dentist appointment because she was nervous.
- The team put off the launch date after the software bugs were discovered.
- Can we put off the meeting until next week?
- He always puts off filing his taxes until the very last minute.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- put off a meeting
- put off the decision
- put off until later
- put off for good
- put off the deadline

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Avoid mixing up this phrasal verb with the meaning 'to discourage' (e.g., 'The bad review put me off buying the product'). In the postponement sense, it is followed by an object and often a time expression.

**Natural alternatives:**

- postpone
- delay
- defer
- table (in U.S. business talk)

# 5. FUEL

## FRONT

**fuel**

“These rumors fuel the argument.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

to make a disagreement, debate, or feeling stronger or more intense

**Category:**

verb

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/ˈfjuːəl/ – stress on the first syllable; the /j/ sound links smoothly to the following vowel in “fuel the”.

**More examples:**

- The scandal fueled the public's outrage.
- His sarcastic comment only fueled the tension in the meeting.
- Social media posts are fueling the spread of misinformation.
- A good night’s sleep can fuel your productivity the next day.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- fuel the fire
- fuel speculation
- fuel a debate
- fuel growth
- fuel momentum

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Avoid using “fuel” with a literal object when you mean the figurative sense; e.g., say “fuel the argument” not “fuel the argument’s”. The verb works best with nouns that describe a conflict, feeling, or process.

**Natural alternatives:**

- intensify
- stoke
- exacerbate
- propel
