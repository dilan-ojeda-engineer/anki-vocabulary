# 1. AWKWARD

## FRONT

**awkward**

“I felt awkward asking for help.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

Feeling uncomfortable or self-conscious in a social situation, especially when you are not sure how to act or what to say.

**Category:**

adjective

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

IPA: /ˈɔːkwərd/. Stress falls on the first syllable. The 'aw' is a long open back vowel, the 'w' is a clear labial glide, and the final 'rd' cluster requires a slight tongue movement from the r to the d. In connected speech the word is usually fully pronounced because it often ends a clause or is followed by a pause.

**For Spanish speakers:**

The 'aw' sound is similar to the Spanish 'o' but more open and rounded; practice it as in the English word 'law'. The 'w' is a clear /w/ glide, not a 'v' or 'b'. The 'r' in 'ward' is the American English r: the tongue curls back without touching the roof of the mouth, unlike the Spanish tapped or trilled r. The final 'd' is fully pronounced, unlike in Spanish where final consonants are often weakened or dropped.

**More examples:**

- There was an awkward silence after he told the joke.
- She felt awkward sitting next to her ex at the party.
- It was an awkward moment when the Wi-Fi password didn't work.
- He made an awkward comment that made everyone uncomfortable.
- I found it awkward to talk about money with friends.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- awkward silence
- awkward moment
- feel awkward
- awkward pause
- awkward situation

**Common mistake and usage note:**

In everyday speech 'awkward' most often describes social discomfort or uncertainty about how to behave. It is not the same as 'uncomfortable': awkward specifically implies social tension or not knowing what to do. A common learner mistake is using 'awkward' to mean 'difficult' in a general sense (e.g., 'an awkward problem'); in that case 'tricky' or 'difficult' is more natural.

**Natural alternatives:**

- I felt self-conscious asking for help.
- It was an uncomfortable situation.
- I felt out of place asking for help.

**Spanish Translation:**

Me sentí incómodo pidiendo ayuda.

# 2. DRAINING

## FRONT

**draining**

“Back-to-back meetings can be draining.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

Exhausting; depleting your physical or mental energy. Used as an adjective to describe something that leaves you feeling tired and empty of energy.

**Category:**

adjective (participial)

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

Stress falls on the first syllable: DREY-ning. The /eɪ/ diphthong is clear and open, like the 'a' in 'day'. The final /ŋ/ is a velar nasal — your tongue touches the back of the roof of your mouth, not the tip. Avoid adding a hard 'g' sound after the 'n'.

**For Spanish speakers:**

The 'dr' cluster requires practice: start with a light 'd' and let the 'r' roll or tap naturally without over-pronouncing it. The /eɪ/ diphthong is not the same as Spanish 'e' or 'ai' — open your mouth wider and glide from a mid-front vowel to a back vowel. The final /ŋ/ is similar to the 'n' you hear before 'g' or 'k' in words like 'inglés' or 'enorme' — keep your tongue at the back of the mouth and do not release with a 'g' sound.

**More examples:**

- That project was mentally draining — I needed a full weekend to recover.
- Caring for a sick parent can be emotionally draining.
- The long commute is physically draining, so I try to listen to podcasts on the way.
- She said the job was draining her energy, so she started looking for something less intense.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- emotionally draining
- mentally draining
- physically draining
- energy-draining
- draining effect
- draining experience

**Common mistake and usage note:**

In this sense, 'draining' functions as an adjective and is typically used with 'be' (is, was, are, can be). Do not confuse it with the literal meaning of liquid flowing out. A common learner error is using it as a transitive verb in the adjective sense: say 'The meeting was draining' rather than 'The meeting drained me' when you mean it felt exhausting (though 'drained me' is also correct as a verb meaning 'took my energy').

**Natural alternatives:**

- exhausting
- wearing
- depleting
- fatiguing

**Spanish Translation:**

Las reuniones consecutivas pueden ser agotadoras.
