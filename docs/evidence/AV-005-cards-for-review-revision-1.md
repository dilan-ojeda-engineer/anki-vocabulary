# P01. awkward

## FRONT

**awkward**

“I felt awkward asking for help.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

feeling uneasy or embarrassed in a situation

**Category:**

Adjective

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/ˈɑːkwɚd/; /ˈɔːkwɚd/ is also used by American speakers. Stress the first syllable. The second syllable has an American r-colored vowel; do not use the vowel in cat.

**For Spanish speakers:**

La primera vocal no es /æ/ como en cat. En la segunda sílaba, mantén la r americana sin vibrarla como una r española. La aproximación escrita no sustituye al IPA ni al audio.

**More examples:**

- She felt awkward staying silent during the meeting.
- It was an awkward moment when the power went out.
- He gave an awkward laugh after the joke fell flat.
- They had an awkward conversation about money.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- feel awkward
- an awkward silence
- an awkward moment
- an awkward conversation

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Use feel awkward to describe feeling uncomfortable in a situation. Awkward can also describe something difficult to use or handle; it is not limited to people or social situations.

**Natural alternatives:**

- uneasy
- uncomfortable
- embarrassed

**Spanish Translation:**

incómodo / sentirse incómodo

# P02. draining

## FRONT

**draining**

“Back-to-back meetings can be draining.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

causing you to feel very tired or mentally exhausted

**Category:**

Adjective, from the regular verb drain

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/ˈdreɪ.nɪŋ/ – stress on the first syllable; the “a” sounds like the “a” in “day”, and the final “‑ing” is pronounced with a short /ɪŋ/.

**For Spanish speakers:**

Empieza con /dr/ y el diptongo /eɪ/. En -ing, /ɪ/ no es la i española: cambia la posición de la lengua, no solo la duración. Termina con /ŋ/, sin añadir una g separada.

**More examples:**

- The long commute was really draining after work.
- Studying for finals can be draining if you don’t take breaks.
- She found the constant noise in the office draining.
- Running a marathon without proper training is draining.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- emotionally draining
- draining experience
- draining job
- draining schedule
- draining workload

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Learners often mix up “draining” (describing the thing that causes fatigue) with “drained” (describing how a person feels). Use “draining” for the source of exhaustion.

**Natural alternatives:**

- exhausting
- tiring
- wearing me out
- taking a lot out of me

**Spanish Translation:**

agotador

# P03. went

## FRONT

**went**

“I went to the office yesterday.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

past tense of go; traveled or moved to a place

**Category:**

Verb — past simple of go

**Irregular Forms:**

go → went → gone

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/wɛnt/. The vowel is like the one in bet. Keep the final /nt/ together without adding a vowel; the final t may be unreleased in natural speech.

**For Spanish speakers:**

Empieza con /w/, sin añadir una g. La vocal /ɛ/ es más abierta que la e española. Conserva /nt/ al final y no añadas una e después de la t.

**More examples:**

- She went to the gym after work.
- We went out for dinner last Friday.
- They went to the office early to finish the paperwork.
- He went back home to get his laptop.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- went to work
- went to the office
- went home
- went back
- went out for dinner

**Common mistake and usage note:**

Common mistake: using “go” instead of the past form “went” when talking about a completed action. Remember “went” is only for past events.

**Natural alternatives:**

- traveled to
- headed to
- went over to

**Spanish Translation:**

fui

# P04. put off

## FRONT

**put off**

“Let's not put off this conversation.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

to postpone or delay something

**Category:**

Phrasal verb

**Irregular Forms:**

put → put → put

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/pʊt ˈɔf/; off also has /ɑf/ in American speech. Link the final t into the next vowel. Phrase stress can change with emphasis; do not assume put is always stressed.

**For Spanish speakers:**

Empieza put con /p/, no con /s/. La vocal /ʊ/ no es exactamente la u española. En off, mantén /f/ al final sin añadir una vocal.

**More examples:**

- We put off the meeting until next week.
- She keeps putting off her dentist appointment.
- I’ve put it off long enough.
- Don’t put off talking to your manager.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- put off the deadline
- put it off
- put off until later
- put off the decision

**Common mistake and usage note:**

With a pronoun, put it between the two parts: put it off, not put off it. A noun can follow off or go between the parts: put off the meeting / put the meeting off.

**Natural alternatives:**

- postpone
- delay
- leave it until later

**Spanish Translation:**

posponer

# P05. fuel

## FRONT

**fuel**

“These rumors fuel the argument.”

**What does this expression mean?**

## BACK

**Meaning:**

to make something stronger or more intense, especially a disagreement or debate

**Category:**

Verb

**Pronunciation tip:**

**Native English:**

/ˈfjuːəl/. Start with /fj/ and move from /uː/ toward the weak vowel /ə/ before l. Some speakers compress the syllables; do not describe the stressed vowel as /ʊ/ in book.

**For Spanish speakers:**

Empieza con /f/ seguida de un deslizamiento /j/, como la y inicial inglesa de yes. Pasa de /uː/ hacia una vocal débil antes de la l; una aproximación española no reproduce todos los sonidos.

**More examples:**

- The new evidence fueled the debate.
- His angry speech fueled the crowd’s frustration.
- Social media can fuel rumors.
- The team’s recent win has fueled hopes for a championship.

**Common collocations/chunks:**

- fuel the argument
- fuel the debate
- fuel speculation
- fuel someone’s anger

**Common mistake and usage note:**

In this context, fuel is a verb: something fuels an argument, fear, or speculation by making it stronger. The regular American past spelling is fueled. The noun fuel also means a substance used for energy.

**Natural alternatives:**

- make it stronger
- feed
- add to

**Spanish Translation:**

alimentar / avivar (una discusión o un sentimiento)
